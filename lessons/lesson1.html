What Is Health Science?

01 · Introduction

What Is Health Science?

Health science is the study of health, the human body, disease, medicine, healthcare, and the many scientific processes that help us understand and improve human health.

It is not just one subject. Health science is a huge group of scientific fields that work together. Biology, anatomy, physiology, genetics, microbiology, pharmacology, psychology, neuroscience, public health, medicine, and many other subjects can all be part of health science.

Imagine the human body as a massive puzzle. One scientist might study a single type of cell. Another might study how the heart pumps blood. Another might study how genes influence disease. Another might study how a medicine changes the behavior of cells. Each person is studying a different piece of the same puzzle.

💡 Simple definition Health science is the scientific study of the human body, health, disease, prevention, treatment, and healthcare.
Health A state of physical, mental, and social well-being.
Science A systematic way of studying the natural world using observation, evidence, testing, and reasoning.
Medicine The field concerned with preventing, diagnosing, treating, and managing disease and health conditions.
Healthcare The services and systems used to maintain or improve people's health.
02 · The Human Body

The Human Body Is One Connected System

One of the most important ideas in health science is that the human body is not made of completely separate parts. It is an interconnected system.

Your heart, lungs, brain, kidneys, digestive system, immune system, muscles, bones, blood vessels, hormones, and cells constantly communicate and influence one another.

For example, your lungs bring oxygen into your body. Oxygen enters the bloodstream and is transported to cells. Cells use oxygen during cellular respiration to help produce usable energy. The heart helps circulate the blood, while the nervous and endocrine systems help regulate many of these processes.

This means that a problem in one part of the body can sometimes affect other parts. Understanding health therefore requires understanding connections rather than memorizing isolated facts.

Human anatomy is one of the foundations of health science.
🧠 Think about it
If your cells need oxygen, but your lungs cannot bring in enough oxygen, your bloodstream cannot deliver enough oxygen to tissues. One problem can therefore affect several systems at once.
03 · Scientific Thinking

Why Is Health Science a Science?

Health science is based on evidence. Scientists and healthcare professionals do not simply decide that something is true because it sounds reasonable. They collect information, make observations, perform experiments or studies, analyze results, and compare evidence.

Scientific knowledge can change when better evidence becomes available. This is not a weakness of science. It is one of the reasons science is useful. Science allows people to investigate questions and update explanations when new evidence provides a better understanding.

The scientific process

  1. Ask a question.
  2. Research what is already known.
  3. Develop a possible explanation or hypothesis.
  4. Collect evidence through observation or research.
  5. Analyze the evidence.
  6. Draw conclusions.
  7. Share the findings so other researchers can evaluate them.
🔬 Evidence matters A scientific claim is stronger when it is supported by reliable, repeatable evidence from well-designed research.
04 · Fields

Major Areas of Health Science

Health science contains many different areas of study. These fields overlap, which is why healthcare often requires people with different types of expertise to work together.

Biology

Biology is the study of living organisms. It provides many of the basic concepts needed to understand human health, including cells, tissues, organs, genetics, metabolism, and evolution.

Anatomy

Anatomy is the study of the structure of the body. It asks questions such as where structures are located, what they look like, and how different structures are arranged.

Physiology

Physiology is the study of how living organisms and their body parts function. For example, physiology can examine how the heart contracts, how neurons communicate, or how the kidneys regulate the internal environment.

Microbiology

Microbiology focuses on microscopic organisms and biological agents, including bacteria, fungi, and other microorganisms. Understanding microorganisms is especially important when studying infection and disease.

Genetics

Genetics is the study of genes, heredity, DNA, and how biological information is passed between generations. Genetics can help explain why certain characteristics or diseases run in families.

Pharmacology

Pharmacology is the study of drugs and how they interact with living organisms. It includes understanding how medicines enter the body, what they do, and how the body processes them.

Psychology and Neuroscience

Psychology studies behavior and mental processes, while neuroscience studies the nervous system and brain. These areas help us understand how biological processes and experiences can influence thoughts, behavior, learning, emotions, and health.

05 · Biology

Biology: One of the Foundations

Biology is one of the most important foundations of health science because humans are living organisms. Before studying disease or medicine, it helps to understand how living systems work.

At the smallest levels, scientists can study molecules and atoms. Molecules form structures inside cells. Cells combine to form tissues. Tissues form organs. Organs work together in organ systems, and organ systems work together to maintain the body.

This organization creates different levels of biological complexity. Later in this course, you will study these levels in much more detail.

Cell The basic structural and functional unit of living organisms.
Tissue A group of similar cells working together.
Organ A structure made of multiple tissues that performs specific functions.
Organ System A group of organs that work together to perform major body functions.
06 · Anatomy & Physiology

Anatomy vs. Physiology

Anatomy and physiology are closely connected, but they are not exactly the same subject.

🦴 Anatomy = Structure Anatomy asks:

What is it?
Where is it?
What does it look like?
How is it organized?
❤️ Physiology = Function Physiology asks:

What does it do?
How does it work?
How is its function regulated?

For example, anatomy can describe the chambers of the heart and the location of the valves. Physiology can explain how those chambers contract and how the valves help blood move in the correct direction.

⭐ Easy way to remember Anatomy = what something is and where it is.
Physiology = what something does and how it works.
07 · Genetics

Genetics and Health

Genetics is another major part of health science. Your DNA contains biological instructions that influence how cells build proteins and carry out their functions.

Genes are sections of DNA that contain information used by cells. Different versions of genes can contribute to differences between people. Some genetic changes can also contribute to disease.

Genetics does not mean that every health outcome is predetermined. Many health conditions are influenced by a combination of genes, environment, behavior, development, and other biological factors.

🧬 Important idea
Genes can influence health, but genes are only one part of the complicated system that affects a person's health.
08 · Medicine

Pharmacology and Medicine

Medicines are another major area of health science. Pharmacology helps scientists understand how drugs interact with cells, tissues, organs, and biological molecules.

Scientists may study how a drug enters the body, how it reaches its target, how it produces an effect, how the body changes the drug, and how the drug eventually leaves the body.

Developing a new medicine is a long scientific process. Before many medicines can become widely available, researchers need evidence about their effectiveness, safety, appropriate dosing, and possible side effects.

Healthcare often combines knowledge from many different areas of health science.
09 · Careers

Health Science Careers

Health science can lead to many different careers. Not everyone who works in health science is a doctor or nurse. Healthcare and medical research require people with many different skills.

  • Physicians diagnose and manage health conditions.
  • Nurses provide patient care, monitor patients, educate patients, and coordinate many aspects of healthcare.
  • Pharmacists specialize in medicines and medication-related care.
  • Medical laboratory scientists analyze biological samples and perform laboratory testing.
  • Researchers investigate biological questions, diseases, treatments, and new technologies.
  • Epidemiologists study patterns of disease and health across populations.
  • Psychologists study behavior, mental processes, and psychological health.
  • Genetic counselors help people understand genetic information and its possible health implications.

These careers may look very different, but they share a common foundation: using knowledge and evidence to understand health and help people.

10 · Connecting It All

How Everything Connects

Health science becomes much easier to understand when you stop thinking of each subject as a completely separate topic.

Consider a person who develops an infection. Microbiology can help identify the microorganism. Immunology can help explain the body's response. Anatomy can describe which organs are involved. Physiology can explain how those organs are functioning. Genetics may influence susceptibility. Pharmacology can help explain how a medicine might work against the infection.

All of those fields are looking at different parts of the same biological problem.

🧠 The big picture Health science is a network of connected subjects. Learning one area often makes another area easier to understand.

Physiology The study of biological function.
Genetics The study of genes, heredity, and biological information.

14 · Key Takeaways

What You Should Remember

  • Health science is the scientific study of health, disease, the body, medicine, and healthcare.
  • Health science contains many different fields that work together.
  • Anatomy focuses on structure.
  • Physiology focuses on function.
  • Genetics studies genes, heredity, and biological information.
  • Microbiology studies microorganisms.
  • Pharmacology studies drugs and their effects.
  • Scientific knowledge depends on evidence and can change when stronger evidence becomes available.
  • Understanding health requires looking at how different parts of the body and different scientific fields connect.
