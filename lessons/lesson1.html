What Is Health Science?

Welcome to Lesson 1 of MedNotesLab! In this lesson, you'll learn what health science actually means, why it matters, how different areas of science work together, and how health science connects to real people, medicine, research, and healthcare.

🌸 1. Start Here: What Is Health Science?

Health science is the study of health, the human body, disease, medicine, healthcare, and the many scientific processes that help us understand and improve human health.

Think about everything that has to happen to keep one person alive. Your heart has to pump blood. Your lungs have to exchange gases. Your digestive system has to break food down. Your kidneys have to filter your blood. Your immune system has to recognize harmful organisms. Your brain has to receive and process information.

All of these processes are connected. That is one of the most important ideas in health science: the human body is not made of completely separate parts. It is a collection of systems that constantly communicate and work together.

Health science: A broad field of science that studies health, the human body, disease, medicine, healthcare, prevention, treatment, and the factors that influence human well-being.

Health science is also much bigger than becoming a doctor. Scientists, nurses, pharmacists, therapists, laboratory professionals, researchers, geneticists, public health professionals, psychologists, and many other people contribute to healthcare.

Human anatomy model showing structures of the human body.

🫀 2. The Human Body Is One Big System

One of the easiest ways to understand health science is to imagine your body as a giant network.

Your heart does not work completely independently. It depends on the lungs to receive oxygen. The lungs depend on the nervous system and muscles to control breathing. The muscles need oxygen and nutrients delivered by the blood. The brain needs a constant supply of oxygen and glucose. The kidneys help control the composition of the blood that reaches all of these organs.

This means that something affecting one part of the body can sometimes affect another part.

💡 Think about it: If the lungs cannot bring enough oxygen into the body, the cardiovascular system has less oxygen to deliver. The brain can also be affected because brain cells need oxygen to function normally.

Health scientists study these relationships so that they can understand what is happening inside the body and determine how different conditions may affect health.

Major body systems

  • Nervous system: communicates and processes information.
  • Cardiovascular system: moves blood throughout the body.
  • Respiratory system: helps exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide.
  • Digestive system: breaks food down and absorbs nutrients.
  • Urinary system: helps remove waste and regulate body fluids.
  • Immune system: protects the body from harmful threats.
  • Endocrine system: produces hormones that regulate body processes.
  • Muscular system: allows movement and helps maintain posture.
  • Skeletal system: provides support and protects organs.

🔬 3. Why Is Health Science a Science?

Health science uses the scientific method to investigate questions about health and the human body.

Scientists do not simply decide that something is true because it sounds reasonable. They collect evidence, make observations, conduct experiments or studies, analyze information, and compare results.

Scientific evidence: Information collected through systematic observation, measurement, research, experimentation, or other reliable scientific methods.

The basic idea

  1. Ask a question.
  2. Research what is already known.
  3. Create a hypothesis or prediction.
  4. Collect evidence.
  5. Analyze the results.
  6. Draw conclusions.
  7. Share the findings so others can evaluate them.

This process is extremely important in medicine because incorrect information can affect real people's health.

⚠️ Important: A health claim being popular online does not automatically make it scientifically supported. Health science relies on evidence rather than popularity.

🧪 4. The Major Areas of Health Science

Health science includes many different scientific fields. Each field asks different questions, but they often overlap.

Biology

Biology is the study of living things. It helps scientists understand cells, DNA, tissues, organs, organisms, and biological processes.

Anatomy

Anatomy is the study of the structures of the body.

Anatomy: The study of body structures and where those structures are located.

Physiology

Physiology focuses on how those structures function.

Physiology: The study of how living organisms and their body parts function.

Genetics

Genetics studies genes, DNA, heredity, and how biological characteristics can be passed from parents to offspring.

Microbiology

Microbiology studies microscopic organisms and biological agents such as bacteria, fungi, and viruses.

Pharmacology

Pharmacology studies drugs and how substances interact with living organisms.

Psychology and neuroscience

Psychology studies behavior and mental processes, while neuroscience studies the nervous system and brain. These fields can overlap when scientists study how the brain influences behavior, emotions, learning, and health.

🧬 5. Biology: The Foundation

Biology is one of the foundations of health science because humans are living organisms.

To understand disease, researchers often need to understand what normally happens inside healthy cells and tissues first.

For example, if scientists want to understand cancer, they need to understand how normal cells grow, communicate, divide, and eventually stop dividing. They can then investigate what changes when those processes become abnormal.

This is why MedNotesLab begins with foundations. Learning individual facts is useful, but understanding how those facts connect makes later topics much easier.

📌 Study strategy: When learning a new medical topic, ask yourself: “What normally happens?” Then ask: “What changes when something goes wrong?”

🦴 6. Anatomy vs. Physiology

Anatomy and physiology are closely connected, but they are not the same thing.

Anatomy = structure
What something is, what it looks like, where it is, and what it is connected to.
Physiology = function
What something does and how it works.

Imagine the heart. Anatomy would involve studying the heart's chambers, valves, walls, blood vessels, and location in the chest.

Physiology would involve studying how the heart contracts, how electrical signals coordinate the heartbeat, and how blood moves through the chambers.

Structure and function are connected. The shape and organization of a body part often help it perform its job.

🧬 7. Genetics and Health

Your body contains DNA, a molecule that stores biological information. Segments of DNA called genes provide instructions that influence many biological processes.

DNA: A molecule that stores genetic information used by living organisms.
Gene: A segment of DNA that contains information used to produce a functional product, such as a protein or functional RNA.

Genetics is important to health science because differences in genes can sometimes influence a person's traits, disease risk, or response to certain medications.

However, genes are not the only factor affecting health. Environment, behavior, nutrition, infections, access to healthcare, and many other factors can also influence health outcomes.

💊 8. Pharmacology and Medicine

Medicine involves much more than knowing the names of medications. Scientists need to understand how drugs interact with cells, tissues, organs, and biological systems.

Pharmacology helps answer questions such as:

  • How does a drug enter the body?
  • Where does it travel?
  • What does it interact with?
  • How does the body change or remove it?
  • What effects can it produce?

Before many medical treatments become widely available, they go through research and clinical testing designed to evaluate their effects and safety.

Health science includes an enormous number of careers.

  • Medical laboratory scientists
  • Physical therapists

Some professionals work directly with patients. Others work in laboratories, research institutions, public health organizations, universities, technology, or other areas.

🔗 10. How All These Fields Connect

The most important idea from this lesson is that health science is interconnected.

Imagine a person develops a disease.

A researcher might study the disease at the cellular level. A geneticist might investigate whether genetic factors are involved. An anatomist might study which organs are affected. A physiologist might investigate how those organs are functioning. A pharmacologist might study possible treatments. A physician might diagnose the patient. A pharmacist might help manage medications. Public health professionals might study how the disease affects a larger population.

Each person is studying a different piece of the same larger problem.

🌸 Big idea: Health science is not one single subject. It is a network of scientific fields that work together to understand, prevent, diagnose, and treat health problems.

🌷 14. Key Takeaways

  • Health science is the broad study of health, disease, medicine, and healthcare.
  • The human body is made of interconnected systems.
  • Biology provides an important foundation for understanding health.
  • Anatomy focuses on structure.
  • Physiology focuses on function.
  • Genetics studies DNA, genes, and heredity.
  • Scientific evidence helps researchers make reliable conclusions.
  • Health science includes many different careers.
  • Different health science fields work together to understand health problems.
