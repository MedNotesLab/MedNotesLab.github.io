READING 01

What Is Health Science?

Health science is the broad study of health, the human body, disease, medicine, and the scientific processes used to understand and improve health. It is not one single subject. Instead, it is a collection of connected scientific and healthcare fields. A person studying health science may eventually learn about cells, organs, genetics, microorganisms, medications, mental health, public health, medical research, and many other topics.

One reason health science is so broad is that the human body is extremely interconnected. The body is made of many different structures, but those structures do not operate independently. Cells form tissues, tissues contribute to organs, and organs work together in organ systems. At the same time, systems communicate with one another through signals, chemicals, electrical activity, blood flow, and other processes. Understanding one part of the body often requires understanding how it interacts with other parts.

Health science also involves understanding health and disease. Scientists and healthcare professionals study what allows the body to function normally and what happens when normal processes are disrupted. For example, researchers might investigate how a particular virus enters cells, how the immune system responds, why a genetic change affects a protein, or how a medication interacts with its target. These questions can involve several different areas of science at the same time.

Another important part of health science is evidence. Health-related ideas should not simply be accepted because someone says they are true. Researchers collect observations, perform experiments or studies when appropriate, analyze data, and compare findings with existing evidence. The goal is to build reliable knowledge about how biological systems work and how health-related questions can be investigated.

This means health science is both about learning the human body and learning how we know what we know about the human body. You will eventually study anatomy and physiology, but you will also study genetics, microbiology, research methods, statistics, disease processes, and medical evidence. These subjects may look separate at first, but they become much more connected as you learn more.

⭐ Key Ideas

  • Health science is a broad group of connected fields.
  • The human body is interconnected rather than a collection of isolated parts.
  • Health science studies both normal body function and disease.
  • Scientific evidence is important when investigating health questions.
  • Understanding health science includes understanding how scientific knowledge is produced.

READING 02

Understanding Health

The word health can sound simple, but health is broader than simply “not being sick.” Human health involves many aspects of life and can change over time. A person can experience changes in physical condition, emotional state, relationships, surroundings, or access to resources that influence health.

Physical health relates to the condition and functioning of the body. It includes processes such as circulation, breathing, digestion, movement, immune function, and the maintenance of internal conditions. Physical health does not mean that a person must have a perfectly functioning body at every moment. Bodies naturally change, and illness or injury can occur. Studying physical health means understanding how biological systems function and what factors can affect them.

Mental health relates to emotional, psychological, and cognitive well-being. The brain influences thoughts, emotions, behavior, learning, memory, and communication. Mental and physical processes are also connected. For example, the nervous and endocrine systems can influence many physical processes, while physical conditions can affect how a person feels and functions.

Social health involves relationships and interaction with other people. Humans are social organisms, and relationships, communication, support systems, and community environments can all influence health. Social health is therefore relevant to health science even though it cannot be reduced to a single organ or body structure.

Environmental factors can also influence health. Air quality, water, housing, workplace conditions, exposure to hazards, access to nutritious food, and many other environmental circumstances can affect people and communities. Public health researchers often examine these larger factors because health does not occur in isolation from the environment.

These dimensions should not be treated as completely separate boxes. They can influence one another. A person's biological condition can affect daily activities, while stress or environmental conditions can influence biological processes. This interconnectedness is one reason health science includes many different disciplines.

⭐ Key Ideas

  • Health is broader than simply the absence of disease.
  • Physical, mental, social, and environmental factors can interact.
  • The brain and body communicate continuously.
  • Environmental conditions can affect individual and population health.
  • Health is multidimensional and interconnected.

READING 03

Anatomy, Physiology, and the Difference Between Structure and Function

Two of the most important subjects in health science are anatomy and physiology. Anatomy is the scientific study of body structures. Physiology is the scientific study of how those structures function. The two subjects are closely connected because the structure of something often helps explain what it can do.

Anatomy can be studied at many different scales. Gross anatomy examines structures that can generally be seen without a microscope, such as organs and major body regions. Microscopic anatomy examines structures that require magnification. This includes cytology, the study of cells, and histology, the study of tissues.

Physiology asks a different type of question. Instead of asking only “What is this structure?”, physiology asks questions such as “What does this structure do?”, “How does it work?”, and “How does it interact with other structures?” For example, anatomy can describe the physical structure of the heart, while physiology can explain how the heart contracts and moves blood through the circulatory system.

Structure and function are therefore connected. Consider the human hand. The arrangement of the bones, joints, muscles, tendons, and nerves allows the hand to perform precise movements. The thumb's position relative to the fingers contributes to the ability to grasp and manipulate objects. Understanding the structure makes the function easier to understand.

This relationship appears throughout biology. The shape of a cell can help it perform its specialized function. The structure of a blood vessel affects how blood moves through it. The organization of nervous tissue allows neurons to communicate. As you continue through MedNotesLab, you will repeatedly see the same pattern: structure and function are connected.

⭐ Key Ideas

  • Anatomy = structure.
  • Physiology = function.
  • Gross anatomy studies larger structures.
  • Microscopic anatomy studies structures requiring magnification.
  • Structure often helps explain function.

READING 04

Major Fields of Health Science

Because health is complicated, health science contains many specialized fields. Each field focuses on certain questions, but the fields often overlap. A researcher studying a disease might need information about cells, genetics, microorganisms, physiology, medications, and population health at the same time.

Anatomy studies body structures, while physiology studies how those structures function. These subjects provide the foundation for understanding what the body is made of and how it operates.

Genetics focuses on heredity and biological information passed between generations. Modern genetics also examines DNA, genes, gene expression, mutations, and how genetic information contributes to biological traits and disease.

Microbiology studies microscopic organisms and biological agents such as bacteria, fungi, and other microscopic life forms. Microbiology is especially important for understanding infectious disease and interactions between microorganisms and their hosts.

Psychology and neuroscience examine behavior, mental processes, and the nervous system from different perspectives. Neuroscience focuses strongly on the nervous system, including the brain, spinal cord, and communication between nerve cells.

Pharmacology studies drugs and how substances interact with biological systems. Researchers may investigate how a drug reaches its target, how it produces an effect, and how the body processes the substance.

Public health looks beyond one individual and studies health at the population or community level. Public health can involve disease prevention, environmental factors, health behavior, epidemiology, and systems that influence communities.

Medical research uses scientific methods to investigate health-related questions. Researchers may study basic biological mechanisms, diseases, treatments, diagnostic methods, prevention strategies, or population-level patterns.

The important idea is that these fields are connected rather than isolated. A medical question may require multiple perspectives. Understanding how those perspectives fit together is one of the central skills you will develop throughout this course.

⭐ Key Ideas

  • Health science contains many specialized fields.
  • Genetics studies heredity and biological information.
  • Microbiology studies microscopic organisms and biological agents.
  • Pharmacology studies how drugs interact with biological systems.
  • Public health studies health at the population and community level.
  • Medical research investigates health-related questions using scientific methods.

READING 05

How the Fields Connect

Imagine that researchers are investigating an infectious disease. There is not one single question they need to answer. They may first need to understand which microorganism causes the disease. That is where microbiology becomes important. They may then investigate how the microorganism enters cells and how those cells respond. Cell biology and physiology can help answer those questions.

Researchers may also ask why some people are more susceptible than others. Genetics could provide information about inherited biological differences. The immune system and physiology could help explain how the body responds after exposure. Neuroscience could become relevant if the disease affects the nervous system.

If researchers are testing a medication, pharmacology becomes important. Scientists need to understand how the medication interacts with biological targets and how the body processes it. Medical researchers may then design studies to determine whether the treatment is effective and what potential risks or side effects need to be investigated.

Public health researchers may approach the same disease from a different level. Instead of focusing on one patient's cells, they might examine how the disease spreads through a population, identify risk factors, evaluate prevention strategies, or study patterns of illness.

Notice what happened: the same health problem required information from several scientific fields. This is called a multidisciplinary approach. Complex health questions often cannot be fully understood from one perspective. Scientists and healthcare professionals therefore combine different types of knowledge.

This is one of the biggest ideas in health science: the human body is a connected system, and studying health often requires connected areas of science. As you move through MedNotesLab, you will keep returning to this idea.

⭐ Key Ideas

  • Complex health questions often require multiple scientific fields.
  • Different fields can study the same disease from different perspectives.
  • Microbiology, genetics, physiology, pharmacology, and public health can overlap.
  • A multidisciplinary approach combines knowledge from multiple fields.
  • Health science is interconnected because the human body and health are interconnected.
